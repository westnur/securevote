The problem

Organizing a vote takes far more than counting

To decide on a budget, elect a chair or amend a charter, an organizer has to notify participants, check their rights and voting weight, record participation, count the result and prepare the minutes.

Administrative load

Lists, notices, ballots, reminders, checking mandates and writing up results — mostly by hand.

Hard to attend

Participants struggle to attend in person or agree on a time, so reaching quorum is difficult.

Errors & disputes

Questions about quorum, duplicate votes, proxies, voting weight and the correctness of the minutes.

Hard to verify

Afterwards it is difficult to reconstruct who did what and when, whether rules changed, and how the result was calculated.

How it works

One path from agenda to minutes

The whole procedure in one place, following the rules of your organization.

1. Register

Upload the verified participant list and voting weights. The file's fingerprint is logged as the data source.

2. Set up

Agenda item, options, deadline, quorum, majority rule, secret or roll-call ballot.

3. Vote remotely

Participants sign in and vote from any device; repeat voting is blocked.

4. Count

Tally, quorum and majority are calculated automatically by the procedure's rules.

5. Document

Audit report and draft minutes in Kazakh, Russian or English.

Trust & security

What protects the procedure

Protection mechanisms, access control and an audit trail help detect and investigate unauthorized changes.

Blind signatures

Secret ballot

Identification and the content of the vote are separated: the system confirms the right to vote without learning the choice.

Roll-call

Open voting when required

For decisions where each vote must be attributable, choices are linked to the participant — visible only to the organizer.

Hash chain · Merkle

Integrity-protected journal

Every event is chained by SHA-256 hashes and signed by validator nodes, so later edits are detected.

Nullifiers

No repeat voting

Each ballot can be used once; repeat submissions are rejected automatically.

Off-chain PII

Personal data kept separate

Names and contacts stay in a protected database (hosted in Kazakhstan where required); the journal holds only minimal metadata.

Receipts

Participant verification

Each voter gets a private receipt to confirm their ballot was counted, without revealing the choice to others.

What the technology does — and doesn't

✓ Helps to

  • Detect changes to previously recorded entries
  • Publish integrity hashes without publishing documents
  • Record the sequence of events and confirm results
  • Make independent checking possible

✕ Does not by itself guarantee

  • That people, devices or admins were not compromised
  • That the initial participant list and weights are correct
  • That the procedure complies with the law or charter
  • Protection from denial of service, key leaks or social engineering

That is why SecureVote combines the journal with register verification, access control, admin MFA, encryption, backups and procedure templates agreed with the client.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Is the result legally binding?

The tally is produced automatically, but legal significance depends on following the applicable procedure — the law, your charter and the required signatures. Before a pilot with legal consequences we agree with you which procedure is used and what status the result has.

Are voting tokens the same as shares?

No. A token is only a technical representation of the right to vote in one specific procedure, created from the verified participant register and its rules. It does not replace shares or the official register of rights.

How is a secret ballot kept secret?

For secret votes we use blind signatures: after signing in, your browser prepares ballots and the server confirms your right to vote without seeing them. The ballots are then submitted without your identity. Confidentiality is provided according to the rules of each vote and the applicable legal requirements.

Can results be changed after the fact?

Every event is recorded in a hash-chained journal signed by validator nodes. Changing an earlier entry breaks the hashes and signatures, so the change is detected — you can try it with the attack simulation in the ledger explorer.

Where is personal data stored?

Names, contacts and identification data are kept off the journal in a protected database. Kazakhstan's personal-data law can require storage within the country; hosting is chosen accordingly for each client.

Which languages are supported?

Draft minutes and reports are generated in Kazakh, Russian and English. Full interface localisation into Kazakh and Russian is part of the roadmap.
