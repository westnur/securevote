Security architecture

Six layers of cryptographic protection

Every guarantee is enforced by mathematics, not by policy.

RSA-2048

Blind signatures

Ballots are signed while "blinded", so the platform can confirm eligibility yet can never link a vote to a shareholder.

Ed25519 · 2-of-3

Validator consensus

Each block is signed by independent nodes — company, auditor and registrar. No single party can rewrite history.

SHA-256

Hash-chained blocks

Each block commits to the previous one. Changing a single ballot breaks every block that follows.

Merkle

Merkle roots

Transactions are summarised in a Merkle tree, so any edit to any ballot is detected instantly.

Nullifiers

No double voting

Every ballot token carries a one-time nullifier. Re-using a token is rejected by the ledger.

Receipts

End-to-end verifiable

Voters get a private receipt to confirm their ballot is on the public ledger — without revealing who they are.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

How can a vote be anonymous if only shareholders may vote?

We use RSA blind signatures. After you sign in, your browser creates random ballot tokens, "blinds" them and asks the server to sign them. The server checks you are eligible and signs — but it cannot see the tokens. Your browser then removes the blinding and submits the ballots without logging in. The signature proves eligibility; nothing links it back to you.

How are votes weighted by shareholding?

Each share is one voting token. Your tokens are split into denominations (1, 10, 100, 1,000 …) and every token becomes a signed ballot, so a holder of 4,000 shares casts exactly 4,000 votes. You can even split your vote between options.

What stops someone from changing results after the fact?

Every ballot is placed in a block that contains a Merkle root of its transactions and the hash of the previous block, and is signed by three independent validator nodes. Editing anything breaks the hashes and signatures — try it yourself with the tamper simulation in the ledger explorer.

Do shareholders need a crypto wallet?

No. Shareholders sign in with email and password. All cryptography runs automatically in the browser.

Is this legally valid for general meetings?

Electronic voting is permitted for general meetings in many jurisdictions where the company's articles allow it. SecureVote produces an audit report suitable for the minutes; we recommend confirming requirements with your corporate counsel.
